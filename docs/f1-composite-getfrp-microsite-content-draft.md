## 5. Product page draft — Pultruded FRP Structural Profiles

**Proposed eyebrow:** F1-STRUX · STANDARD PULTRUDED SHAPES

# F1 Composites Pultruded FRP Structural Profiles

F1 Composites presents F1-STRUX as its range of standard pultruded fiberglass structural shapes. The published family includes I-beams, channels, equal and unequal angles, square and rectangular tubes, round tubes, flat bars and rods. It is intended for buyers who can begin with a catalog cross-section and then confirm the laminate, dimensions, fabrication and documentation required for the project.

The supplier describes continuous E-glass reinforcement with resin systems selected for the intended exposure. Published options across its technical pages include general-purpose polyester, vinyl ester and fire-retardant formulations. The quoted laminate, rather than the shape name alone, controls final properties and suitability.

> **Evidence status — supplier-reported:** product range and nominal data are summarized from F1 Composites' published catalog and design resources. Confirm the selected size, grade, revision and availability in the quotation.

### Published profile range

- **I-beams:** published depths from approximately 76 to 305 mm, including larger 305 x 305 mm sections.
- **Channels:** published sizes from approximately 38 x 13 mm to 305 x 89 mm.
- **Angles:** equal and unequal legs, from compact 25 x 25 mm profiles to sections around 152 x 152 mm.
- **Hollow profiles:** square and rectangular tubes, with published square sections up to approximately 240 x 240 mm.
- **Round profiles:** hollow tubes with published outside diameters from approximately 25 to 150 mm, plus a separate range of solid rods.
- **Flat bars:** published widths from approximately 12 to 305 mm and thicknesses from approximately 3 to 25 mm.

F1 lists 114 standard profile records. The table is useful for initial geometry screening; the current drawing, nominal mass, section properties, tolerance, resin grade and production status should be checked before design or purchase.

### Where these profiles fit

Pultruded structural shapes are commonly evaluated for industrial platforms, walkway framing, cable support, cooling-tower components, coastal or marine structures, equipment skids, handrail framing and selected solar or infrastructure assemblies. They are most relevant where corrosion resistance, electrical non-conductivity, low maintenance or reduced handling weight can offset the higher material cost or lower elastic modulus relative to steel.

These profiles should not be treated as direct steel substitutions based on outside dimensions alone. Global deflection, transverse properties, local stability, connection bearing, bolt-hole details, sustained loading and environmental reduction factors can govern the design. The project engineer remains responsible for the complete structure and its connections.

### Published technical evidence

For its standard E-glass/isophthalic-polyester laminate, F1 publishes representative values including a 23 GPa longitudinal tensile modulus, 240 MPa longitudinal tensile strength, 240 MPa longitudinal flexural strength and 30 MPa interlaminar shear strength. The supplier compares this standard laminate with the EN 13706 E23 classification.

These are supplier-issued reference values, not batch values for every profile or resin formulation. F1 also refers to ISO 9001-controlled production, EN 13706 and ASTM D3917. Buyers should request current documents naming the applicable entity, product, test method and revision. Planned or described future tests are not current evidence.

### What to include in an RFQ

Provide the catalog profile or drawing, cut length, quantity and required drilling or fabrication. Add the unsupported span, load cases, deflection limit, connection concept, operating temperature, chemicals, UV or weather exposure, resin/fire/color requirements, applicable standards, required CAD or batch documents, destination, Incoterm and target delivery date.

## 6. Product page draft — FRP Grating & Access Systems

# F1 Composites FRP Grating and Access Systems

F1-GRID is GetFRP's buyer-oriented grouping for F1 Composites' pultruded grating, molded grating, closed deck panels, stair products and related access components. These product types can form one coordinated access package, but they are not interchangeable: pultruded grating carries primarily in the bearing-bar direction, molded grating distributes load in two directions, and deck panels create a substantially closed walking surface.

> **Evidence status — supplier-reported:** published dimensions and options are initial-selection data. Final span, load, deflection, slip resistance, resin and fire requirements must be confirmed for the selected construction.

### Product types

- **Pultruded pedestrian grating:** T-bar constructions with published depths of 25 and 38 mm.
- **Pultruded industrial grating:** I-bar constructions with published depths of 25, 30, 38 and 50 mm.
- **Supplier-described high-load grating:** deeper published constructions from approximately 25 to 76 mm for applications requiring greater section depth; the label is not project load approval.
- **High-open-area grating:** published 25 and 38 mm options with nominal open areas of approximately 73% and 83%.
- **Molded grating:** square, mini-mesh and rectangular patterns with published depths from approximately 13 to 65 mm.
- **Closed deck panels:** interlocking or adjacent pultruded panels for a more continuous surface.
- **Stair products:** thin tread covers for an existing substrate and complete grating treads for supported stair construction.
- **Related access components:** round or square handrail members, posts, toe boards, ladders and stainless-steel clips.

Surface choices described by F1 include concave, fine-grit and coarse-grit finishes. Clip letters such as M, J and T are convenient supplier labels, not universal industry standards; the support geometry and fastening detail must accompany the order.

### Where the system fits

This family is most relevant to corrosive, wet or electrically sensitive access areas: chemical and water-treatment plants, cooling towers, coastal or offshore installations, electrical substations and industrial maintenance platforms. Pultruded grating can suit clearly oriented spans and higher directional stiffness. Molded grating can suit complex cut-outs or layouts that benefit from two-way construction. Closed deck panels can be considered where open mesh, falling objects or drainage are undesirable.

The application determines the product. Pedestrian access, concentrated maintenance loads, vehicle or trolley traffic, accessible routes, escape paths and stairways can impose materially different requirements.

### Design and evidence boundaries

F1's pultruded-grating page contains 36 published dimensional rows. GetFRP's internal review found conflicts among the source records, so the web table will be used only for shortlisting; the supplier quotation, approved drawing and order datasheet will control.

No general third-party report has yet been confirmed by GetFRP as proof of load capacity, slip resistance, chemical resistance or fire performance for the entire F1-GRID range. Project approval should instead identify the exact grating type, clear span, design loads, deflection criterion, bearing length, support layout, surface, resin and applicable test evidence. Handrails and complete access structures also require project-specific engineering.

### What to include in an RFQ

State the product type, clear span and bearing direction, distributed and concentrated loads, deflection limit, mesh/open-area or drainage requirements, panel layout, cut-outs and supports. Add the surface and slip requirement, chemical/temperature/UV exposure, resin and fire criteria, clip or fastener geometry, tread nosing, handrail or ladder scope, quantity, destination, Incoterm and required calculations, drawings or inspection records.

## 8. Product page draft — Custom Pultruded Profiles

**Proposed eyebrow:** F1-FORM · ENGINEER-TO-ORDER PULTRUSION

# F1 Composites Custom Pultruded Profiles

F1-FORM is F1 Composites' engineer-to-order route for constant-cross-section parts that do not match a standard catalog profile. The workflow begins with geometry and application review, then moves through reinforcement and resin selection, tooling, trial production, validation and repeat manufacture. It is a development program, not an assurance that every submitted drawing is pultrudable.

The supplier describes glass, carbon, basalt, aramid and hybrid reinforcement options; polyester, vinyl ester, epoxy and polyurethane resin systems; and several surface or veil choices. Final availability, properties and economics depend on the section geometry, reinforcement architecture, surface requirement and production volume.

> **Evidence status — supplier-reported:** public capability figures are screening guides. The approved drawing, material specification, first-article plan and purchase order must define the production requirement.

### Published capability envelope

F1 publishes a maximum section envelope of approximately 600 x 300 mm, wall thicknesses from 1.5 to 30 mm, standard lengths up to 14 m and a typical dimensional tolerance of plus or minus 0.25 mm. These values should not be combined into a guaranteed maximum part: die balance, wall transitions, corners, reinforcement placement, pull force, curing and straightness can reduce the practical envelope for a particular cross-section.

The supplier also publishes indicative minimums of 500 linear metres for first production and 200 metres for repeat orders. Tooling, trial quantities and commercial minimums should be reconfirmed for each program.

### Development route

1. **Application and drawing review:** clarify function, interfaces, load paths, exposure and critical dimensions.
2. **Material architecture:** select resin, reinforcement type and orientation, veil, color and surface finish.
3. **Design for pultrusion:** review uniformity, radii, wall transitions, internal features, tolerances and secondary operations.
4. **Tooling and trial:** manufacture the die, produce trial material and inspect first articles against the agreed plan.
5. **Approval and repeat production:** freeze the controlled drawing and specification, then define batch tests, traceability, packing and change control.

### Where custom pultrusion fits

Custom pultrusion is strongest for repeat-volume parts where corrosion resistance, electrical insulation, thermal performance, low mass or tailored longitudinal stiffness can justify tooling. F1 identifies building and window components, solar members, transportation parts, battery or electrical components, industrial supports and metal-replacement profiles as candidate areas.

The process is anisotropic and optimized for continuous production. Short, highly variable, deeply three-dimensional or heavily cross-loaded parts may be better suited to another composite or metal process. Connections, transverse loads, cut-outs and post-machining need early review.

### Verification and responsibility

F1 describes dimensional and visual checks plus selected tensile, flexural, Barcol-hardness and glass-content testing. The required methods, frequency, sampling, acceptance values and reporting are order-specific. General references to ISO 9001, EN 13706 or ASTM D3917 do not certify every custom part.

Trial and production evidence should be tied to the controlled drawing and laminate. Depending on risk, this may include a first-article inspection, material test reports, section cuts, process or cure records, batch traceability and retained samples. F1's public design resources state that its engineers support product development but do not provide stamped project drawings; the buyer's engineer of record remains responsible for the final application.

### What to include in an RFQ

Send 2D and, where available, 3D files; critical and reference dimensions; tolerances; cut length, straightness and twist requirements; load cases and fiber directions; joints, holes, inserts and machining; chemical, temperature, UV, fire or electrical exposure; preferred fiber, resin, finish and color; prototype and annual volumes; standards, tests and documents; target date, packing, destination and Incoterm. Also define tooling ownership, first-article approval, repair criteria, traceability and change-notification expectations.

## 11. Publication gates

1. **Editorial approval — complete:** the user approved the five-page model, naming, depth and claim labels.
2. **Supplier confirmation:** F1 confirms product-family accuracy, contact details, document-holder roles and any facts that have changed since the source pages were reviewed.
3. **Asset review — complete for initial scope:** the official logo is local and verified; unlicensed product photography is not included.
4. **Evidence review:** every document-backed claim is checked against the original file, entity, configuration, test method and validity date; links resolve without login.
5. **Implementation authorization — complete:** the user approved task-branch implementation of the company page and four product-family pages.
6. **Search-quality gate:** every indexable page has a distinct purpose, useful supplier-specific facts, original GetFRP interpretation, evidence links, an RFQ checklist and non-duplicative imagery.
7. **Schema and policy QA:** structured data matches visible content and excludes unsupported offers, ratings, stock, certifications or guarantees. Claimed, verified and sponsored states remain separate.
8. **Preview QA:** review desktop and mobile layouts, logo containment, links, metadata, canonical URLs, sitemap inclusion, accessibility, performance and the complete claim flow in a Vercel Preview.
9. **Final user approval:** the user approves the preview and any supplier feedback before merge.
10. **Production authorization:** merging to `main` and verifying the production deployment remain a separate, explicit deployment step.
